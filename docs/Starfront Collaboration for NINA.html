Starfront Collaboration for N.I.N.A.

How to take part in a Starfront collaboration from N.I.N.A. with the Starfront Collaboration plugin. For plugin 1.0.0.13, October 2026.

1. Before you start

You need N.I.N.A. 3.2 or later, membership of the Starfront Discord server, and a rig that can plate solve.

  • N.I.N.A. 3.2.0 or later. Help > About shows the version. The plugin runs on 3.2 and 3.3 and will not install on older builds.
  • A Starfront Discord account. Only members of the Starfront Discord server can sign in; there is no separate password or account. If you are not in the server yet, ask in Discord before you start.
  • Plate solving that works. Each field or panel is centred with N.I.N.A.'s own Center instruction, so your plate solver (ASTAP, PlateSolve2, Astrometry.net) must be set up under Options > Plate Solving and working. The Center instruction from the Advanced Sequencer palette is a good test.
  • Your usual profile, connected as usual. Focal length (Options > Equipment > Telescope) and pixel size (camera) must be filled in; the server uses them to work out what your camera covers.
  • Your usual guider, autofocus and meridian-flip arrangements keep working; the plugin only decides where to point and what to shoot. When a guider is connected, every field starts guiding after it is centred, the way a target container does.

2. Install the plugin

The plugin comes from Starfront's own plugin repository, not from the public list, so N.I.N.A. has to be told where to look once.

  1. In N.I.N.A. go to Options > General and find Plugin Repositories.
  2. Press + and enter exactly: 
    https://collab.starfront.space/nina
  3. Go to the Plugins tab (left sidebar) and open Available. Starfront Collaboration is listed; press Install.
  4. Restart N.I.N.A. Plugins only load at start-up.
  5. Afterwards the plugin appears under Plugins > Installed, and updates show there too; install them like any other plugin update.

If Starfront Collaboration is not listed, check the address for typing errors, then press the refresh button on the Plugins tab. The server address must be reachable from the PC; a browser visit to https://collab.starfront.space/api/v1/health should show a short line of text.

3. Sign in with Discord

Signing in once ties this PC's telescope to your Discord account; after that the plugin checks in with the server by itself.

  1. Go to Options > Plugins and select Starfront Collaboration in the list. Its page opens on the right.
  2. Under Account, press Sign in with Discord. Your browser opens on Discord's authorisation page.
  3. Sign in to Discord if asked, then Authorize. Discord only tells Starfront who you are and which server roles you hold; it cannot read your messages.
  4. The browser shows Signed in as <your name>. Go back to N.I.N.A.: the page now says you are signed in and that this telescope is enrolled under your account. Enrolment happens by itself and names the telescope after your N.I.N.A. profile.
  5. Press Check in now once. The status line should read Checked in just now.

If the page says the sign-in code expired, press the button again; you have ten minutes each time. If Discord says you are not a member of the server, join the Starfront Discord first.

Several telescopes? Sign in on each PC. Each gets its own telescope under your account, and the server deals each one its own share. Signing in on a second PC may sign the first one's account out, but the telescope stays enrolled and keeps working; nothing needs redoing.

Sign out forgets the sign-in and the telescope on this PC. Frames already shot stay recorded locally and are reported after the next sign-in.

4. Your filters and exposures

You keep choosing how your frames are exposed; the collaboration only decides which filters tonight and how many frames go where.

On the same options page, under Your exposures, the table lists the filters of your N.I.N.A. filter wheel by their own names. Type the seconds per sub you normally shoot each one at, for example 300 for Ha and 120 for luminance. Your share of every collaboration is dealt at these lengths, so every light calibrates against the darks you already have. The server works out for itself that your "Ha 3nm" is the project's Ha; you never rename anything.

A project may set a shortest or longest sub it will accept. If one of yours falls outside, joining tells you which filter to change, by its wheel name.

5. Join a collaboration

Joining is done from the Starfront panel on the Imaging tab, and it is a decision you make; nothing is ever shot for a project you have not joined.

  1. Open the Imaging tab. In the panel list at the top (the row of icons where Image History and HFR History live) switch on Starfront. It docks like any other panel; drag it where you want it.
  2. The panel shows the server status, who is online, and every open collaboration with:
    • what it is: a single field or a mosaic, its size, and the hours wanted per filter;
    • how far it has got: 23% done - 4.5 h collected, which is the share of the field at full depth, not a total of hours;
    • who is on it and how many of them are online now;
    • whether your telescope qualifies, and if not, why: focal length, star size, a filter too wide, a sub too short;
    • a picture of its sky with the region outlined in yellow, and a graph of the target's altitude through tonight: dusk to dawn, your horizon and the project's floor shaded, transit marked, with one line saying when it is up. Once you have joined, your own panels are drawn on the sky in white and tonight's share in green. Show in Framing Assistant opens N.I.N.A.'s Framing Assistant on the region at the project's angle, with your camera's footprint.
  3. Press Join on a collaboration you qualify for. The server hands your telescope its own part of the sky and, if it is already night, deals tonight's work straight away. The panel's Tonight line shows it, for example M31: the field, 14 x Ha, 10 x OIII or Heart Nebula: 4 panels, 12 x OIII each.
  4. Leave takes you off it. Frames already shot stay credited to the project.

You can be on several collaborations at once; the sequence container shoots all of them, or the one you pick on it.

6. Start your own collaboration

Anybody signed in may start one; being in the Starfront Discord server is the whole gate. Open Start a collaboration at the top of the Starfront panel.

  1. Name it. This is what everybody sees in their list.
  2. Where. Type a name (M31, NGC 7000, Sh2-129) and press Look up; the server fills in the coordinates and, for the objects it knows, suggests a size. Or press From Framing Assistant to take whatever the Framing Assistant is showing, with its rotation. Or type RA and Dec yourself.
  3. One field or a region to cover. One field means everybody points at the same spot and the depth map grows from the union of their cameras. A region is tiled by each telescope with its own camera; give its width and height in degrees.
  4. Filters wanted. One row per filter, pre-filled from your own wheel. Hours wanted is the depth you want at every point of the field; blank means shoot it with no goal. Bandpass keeps wide filters out: 7 under Ha refuses a 12 nm Ha.
  5. Rules are all optional: focal length and exposure ranges, the largest star size and guiding error a sub may have, how bright the Moon may be and how far away, the lowest altitude, the fewest frames a telescope is dealt on a panel in a night, colour cameras yes or no, calibrated frames only.
  6. Start it. It is live at once, in everybody's list. Join it yourself to shoot your share.

A collaboration you started shows Edit, Deal again and Close on its card. Edit changes anything, including the rules; nights already accepted stay accepted, and every telescope on it finds out at its next check-in. Deal again forgets tonight's lists so each telescope is dealt afresh when it next checks in; use it after changing the filters or goals. Close takes it off everybody's list and keeps what was collected on record.

7. Put it in your sequence

One container does the whole night: drop Starfront collaboration where a target would go and keep your usual triggers around it.

  1. Open the Sequencer tab in Advanced mode (the plugin cannot be used from the simple sequencer).
  2. In the instruction palette on the right, find the Starfront category and drag Starfront collaboration into the Targets area of your sequence, where you would normally put a Deep Sky Object container.
  3. On the container, Collaboration chooses what it shoots: Every collaboration I have joined (the default), or one project.
  4. Put the container directly in the Targets area, never inside one of your own Deep Sky Object containers. A trigger on a target container takes its coordinates from that target: one tester's Center After Drift trigger, sitting on an M33 container around the Starfront one, measured 889 arcminutes of "drift" and slewed the mount to M33 in the middle of the M31 run. Nesting also costs a slew and a plate solve to the wrong target at every start.
  5. Triggers that do not care about the target, Autofocus after HFR increase or after time and Meridian Flip, go on the Starfront container itself and fire around every frame. Do not add Center After Drift or Dither after exposures above it. Every field the container builds centres itself, dithers at the interval set in the options, and recentres on its own coordinates if it drifts further than the arcminutes set in the options. You will see that Center After Drift trigger inside each field while it runs; it is the plugin's own and should stay enabled.
  6. Leave the container's own Conditions empty. It runs tonight's deal once and finishes; a Loop until time condition is honoured as a stop, never as a reason to shoot the deal twice.
  7. Keep your Start and End areas as they are: cooling, unparking, guiding start, and parking and warming at the end.

The line under the container shows what the last check-in dealt, and the plugin flags anything missing before you run: not signed in, no exposures set, camera or mount not connected.

A simple arrangement that works:

Start
  Cool camera, Unpark, Start guiding
Targets
  Starfront collaboration     triggers: Meridian Flip, Autofocus after HFR increase
End
  Warm camera, Park

8. What happens during the night

When the sequence reaches the container it asks the server once what this telescope was dealt tonight, then works through it with N.I.N.A.'s own instructions.

The deal. The server has every telescope's part of the sky and everything anyone has already collected. Each night it deals you the frames to put on your field, or on a handful of panels of a mosaic, sized to the hours you said you give. On a mosaic that is one filter for the whole night, narrowband on a bright Moon, broadband or OIII on a dark one. On a single field it is one or more filters, starting with the one the project is furthest behind in against its goal, so the field's depth evens out across the filters; a night cut short loses frames from the deepest filter, not the thinnest. A night's deal holds for the whole night: nothing changes under a running sequence, however many frames arrive from elsewhere at 2 a.m. The next night is dealt afresh from what everyone really collected, and a project that adds a filter deals it to you on the next fresh deal if your wheel carries it.

The run. On a mosaic, if your camera has no rotator and the plugin does not yet know the angle it sits at, or the panels were laid for a different angle, the container first slews to the first panel and plate-solves once; the server then lays your panels again for the real angle before anything is shot. The container builds one target per field or panel, visible in the sequencer as it runs, each containing: is it up?, Slew and center (or Slew, center and rotate with a rotator), Switch filter, Take many exposures. Fields that are up go first, the one that sets soonest at the front; fields still to rise come after. The Starfront panel on the Imaging tab lists them all with where each has got to: done, shooting (frame 4 of 12, or slewing and centring), waiting and when it rises, skipped and why. The list updates with every frame and stays until the next run.

Stopping and starting. A sequence started again during the night carries on where it left off: frames already shot on a field tonight come off its count, and a finished field is not shot again.

Skipping. A field below the project's altitude floor, below your horizon file, or too near a Moon that is up is skipped for now, and the container comes back to it once the others are done. One that never clears the floor in the next twelve hours is left out of tonight and logged.

Your frames. Each light is saved by N.I.N.A. as usual. Its target name is the project's name, plus panel 7 on a mosaic, so your file pattern's $$TARGETNAME$$ sorts them. The FITS header carries STARFRNT (the project) and SFPANEL.

Reporting. Every light is credited to the sky it covered, with its star size and guiding error, kept on this PC, and sent to the server at the next check-in. If the server was down, the frames go the next time it answers; nothing is lost. The server judges each night against the project's rules and the panel shows accepted or rejected, with the reason.

When it ends. The container finishes when the deal is shot, and your End instructions run as normal. If you have time to spare after it, put another target after the container.

9. Checking on it, and when something is wrong

Everything the plugin does is written to N.I.N.A.'s log with the prefix Starfront:, so the log answers most questions.

The log is under %LOCALAPPDATA%\NINA\Logs (paste that into the Windows Explorer address bar); the newest file is tonight's. Search it for Starfront:. The Starfront panel's status line and its Frames reported list show the same things in short.

The plugin never touches your settings, profile or files beyond writing its own under %LOCALAPPDATA%\NINA\PluginSettings. Uninstalling it from the Plugins tab removes it completely.

10. Questions people ask

Can I still choose my exposure times? Yes; that is what the exposure table is. The collaboration decides which filters tonight and how many frames go where, never how a frame is exposed.

I set one hour but it dealt me more. That was a flaw in the server's dealing, fixed on 9 October 2026. A one-hour night now gets about an hour of one filter, the one the project needs most.

Will it move my rotator? Only if one is connected and Rotate to the project's angle is ticked. Without a rotator it shoots at the angle the camera sits and tells the server, so your panels are laid along your camera's axes.

What if clouds stop the night halfway? Whatever was shot is credited and reported. The next night's deal starts from what everyone really collected, so nothing has to be made up by hand.

Can I run my own targets the same night? Yes. Put them before or after the container in the Targets area, with their own conditions. The container only takes the hours the deal is sized to.

Does it see my location? No. The server is told your focal length, pixel size, sensor size, filters, exposures and, if you allow it, where the mount points. Your site, horizon and local times stay on your PC; that is why the plugin works out the Moon and altitudes itself.

I have Starfront and N.I.N.A. on different telescopes. That is fine. Both talk to the same server, and the same person can own telescopes of either kind.

Who do I ask? The Starfront Discord server, where the people running the collaborations are. Say which telescope, and paste the Starfront: lines from the log for the night in question, or the whole log file.

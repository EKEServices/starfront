Getting started with Starfront in N.I.N.A.

A first-night walk-through for people who have not used N.I.N.A.'s Advanced Sequencer before. For plugin 1.0.0.29, October 2026. The full reference is the companion guide Starfront Collaboration for N.I.N.A.

1. What you are building

By the end of this you will have one saved sequence that, every clear night, cools the camera, unparks the mount, shoots whatever the Starfront server dealt your telescope for the collaborations you have joined, then parks and warms up. You load it, press start, and go to bed. Nothing about how you shoot changes: your exposures, your gain, your guider, your autofocus and your meridian flip all stay yours.

The piece that does the work is a single instruction the plugin adds to N.I.N.A., called Starfront collaboration. It sits in the sequence where a target would go. When the sequence reaches it, it asks the server what tonight's share is and shoots it with N.I.N.A.'s own slew, centre, filter and exposure instructions, one field or panel at a time.

2. Ten minutes on the Advanced Sequencer

N.I.N.A. has two sequencers. The simple sequencer is a form: one target, a table of exposures. The Advanced Sequencer is a list of instructions you assemble yourself, and it is the only one the plugin works in. It looks complicated the first time; it is four ideas.

The three areas

Every sequence has three boxes, top to bottom:

  • Start: what to do once at the beginning. Cool the camera, unpark the mount.
  • Targets: the imaging. Normally one Deep Sky Object Instruction Set per target; for Starfront, the Starfront collaboration container goes here instead.
  • End: what to do once at the end. Warm the camera, park the mount.

Instructions, conditions, triggers

Everything you drop into those boxes is one of three kinds, and the palette on the right of the screen (the Instructions sidebar) is sorted by equipment: Camera, Telescope, Filter Wheel, Focuser, Guider, Utility, and so on, plus a Starfront category once the plugin is installed.

  • An instruction does one thing and then the next one runs: Cool Camera, Unpark Scope, Take Many Exposures. Most of the palette is instructions.
  • A condition (Loop Until Time, Loop until Altitude Below) is attached to a box and makes the box repeat its contents for as long as the condition holds. The Starfront container needs none.
  • A trigger (Meridian Flip, AF After HFR Increase, AF After Time) is attached to a box and watches while the box runs, firing between exposures when its moment comes. Triggers are how your meridian flip and autofocus keep working with Starfront.

How things get into the boxes

Drag an item from the palette and drop it into a box. Instructions land in the box's list; a condition or a trigger lands in the box's own Conditions or Triggers row, which appears when there is something in it. You can also press the + on a box to pick an item from a menu. Drag an item up or down to reorder it, and use the small bin icon on an item to remove it. An item inside a box only applies to that box and what is in it.

The buttons at the bottom

Along the bottom of the sequencer are Save Sequence, Save Sequence as new file, Load Sequence, a reset, and on the right the Start Sequence play button (which becomes Cancel Sequence while running). A sequence is a file; you build it once and load it on later nights.

If the Sequencer tab opens on the simple one-target form, press the Advanced Sequencer button at the top right of it. N.I.N.A. remembers which one you used last.

3. Before the first night

Do these once, in the afternoon, with the gear powered up. Each is covered in detail in the companion guide; this is the checklist.

  1. Install the plugin. Options > General > Plugin Repositories > +, enter https://collab.starfront.space/nina, then Plugins > Available > Starfront > Install, and restart N.I.N.A.
  2. Join the Starfront Discord if you have not: discord.com/invite/3ut6a7pfsY. You do not have to be a Starfront customer. Then Options > Plugins > Starfront > Sign in with Discord.
  3. Connect your camera, filter wheel and mount on the Equipment tab, the way you do for any night. The plugin reads the sensor size and pixel size from the connected camera, and the focal length from Options > Equipment > Telescope; the server needs all three to know what your camera covers. If you try to join before the camera has been connected, the plugin says to connect it first.
  4. Fill in the exposure table. On the plugin's options page, under Your exposures, each filter in your wheel has a Delivers column (check the guess once: Ha delivers Ha, a colour camera's clear filter delivers OSC-RGB) and a Seconds per sub column. Type the sub length you normally use for each filter you want to contribute. Leave blank any filter you do not.
  5. Join a collaboration. Imaging tab > switch on the Starfront panel in the panel list at the top > click a collaboration's line to open its card > Join. The card's green line says you can take part and with which filters; an orange one says why not. The panel's Tonight line will show your share once it is dark.

4. Build the sequence, step by step

Open the Sequencer tab in Advanced mode. If there is already something in it, press the reset button at the bottom to start from the three empty boxes. Then work top to bottom.

Start box

1. In the palette, open Camera and drag Cool Camera into Start. On it, type the temperature you normally cool to (for example -10) and a duration in minutes to get there (5 is kind to the camera).

2. Open Telescope and drag Unpark Scope under it.

3. Optional: open Focuser and drag Run Autofocus under that, so the night begins in focus. The Starfront container does not focus for you; your triggers (next) keep it there.

You do not need Start Guiding here. Every field the container shoots starts the guider itself after it is centred, as a normal target does. Having it here as well does no harm.

Targets box

4. In the palette, open the Starfront category and drag Starfront collaboration into Targets. Drop it straight into the Targets box, not inside a Deep Sky Object Instruction Set.

5. On the container, Collaboration is a drop-down: leave it on Every collaboration I have joined, or pick one.

6. Give it your meridian flip: open Telescope in the palette and drag Meridian Flip onto the Starfront container. It lands in the container's Triggers row. Its settings are the ones from Options > Imaging > Meridian Flip, as always.

7. Give it your autofocus: open Focuser and drag AF After HFR Increase (and AF After Time if you use it) onto the container too. They join the same Triggers row and fire between the container's exposures.

8. Do not add Dither after Exposures or Center After Drift to it. The container dithers and recentres each field itself (the interval and the drift limit are on the plugin's options page). While it runs you will see a Center After Drift trigger inside each field it builds; that one is the plugin's own and stays.

9. Leave the container's Conditions row empty. It shoots tonight's deal once and finishes on its own; a Loop Until Time would only ever stop it early, never make it shoot the deal twice.

Under the container, one line tells you what the last check-in dealt and flags anything missing: not signed in, no exposures set, camera or mount not connected. If the line is orange, fix what it names before the night.

End box

10. From Camera drag Warm Camera into End, and from Telescope drag Park Scope under it. If you use a guider, Guider > Stop Guiding before the park is tidy.

The whole thing, read top to bottom:

Start
  Cool Camera (-10 °C, 5 min)
  Unpark Scope
  Run Autofocus            (optional)
Targets
  Starfront collaboration  — Collaboration: Every collaboration I have joined
      Triggers: Meridian Flip, AF After HFR Increase
End
  Stop Guiding
  Warm Camera
  Park Scope

5. Save it so you never build it again

  1. Press Save Sequence as new file at the bottom of the sequencer and name it, for example Starfront night. It is saved as a .json file in your sequence folder.
  2. On any later night, press Load Sequence and pick it. Everything, including the triggers, comes back.
  3. Optional: open the Templates tab of the sidebar and drag the Starfront container (with its triggers) into it. From then on it is one drag from the Templates list into any sequence you build, for example a night that has your own target first and Starfront after.
The sequence does not store which collaborations you are on; the server does. Join or leave on the Starfront panel whenever you like, and the same saved sequence shoots whatever you are on tonight.

6. Run it

  1. Connect everything on the Equipment tab: camera, filter wheel, mount, focuser, guider, rotator if you have one. Unconnected gear shows as a red mark on the instructions that need it.
  2. Open the Imaging tab and look at the Starfront panel's Tonight line. Once it is dark it reads something like M31: the field, 14 x Ha, 10 x OIII. Before dark, or if nothing is dealt, it says so; the container will still ask again when it runs.
  3. Back on the Sequencer tab, press the Start Sequence play button at the bottom right.

Watch it for the first field. The container appears in the sequencer with one sub-container per field or panel, each holding is it up?, Slew and center, Start guiding, Switch filter and Take many exposures, and the running one is highlighted. The Starfront panel on the Imaging tab lists the same fields with where each has got to: shooting frame 4 of 12, waiting to rise, skipped and why. After that, go to bed; the End box runs when the deal is done.

To stop early, press Cancel Sequence. Frames already shot are kept and credited; if you start the sequence again the same night it carries on where it left off.

7. The next morning

  • Your lights are in your usual image folder, saved by N.I.N.A. like any others. The target name on them is the collaboration's name (plus panel 7 on a mosaic), so a file pattern with $$TARGETNAME$$ sorts them into their own folder.
  • Every light was credited to the sky it covered and sent to the server at the next check-in, with its star size and guiding error. The Starfront panel's Frames reported list shows them, and whether the server accepted the night against the collaboration's rules. If the server was unreachable overnight, the frames go the next time it answers.
  • Nothing else to do. Tomorrow's deal is made from what everyone really collected; the next clear night you load the sequence and press start.
